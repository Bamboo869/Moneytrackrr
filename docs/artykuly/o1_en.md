Inflation explained simply: why your PLN 100 is worth less today
Prices go up, and pay doesn't always keep pace. Here is what inflation is, where it comes from and what you can do to feel it less in your wallet – no jargon.
---
Remember what your shopping cost a few years ago? The same basket – bread, milk, butter, coffee – costs noticeably more today. That is inflation: **a general rise in prices across the economy**, which means the same money buys you less.

## What does "4% inflation" mean?

When the statistics office reports 4% inflation, it means the average shopping basket costs 4% more than a year earlier. If your typical shop cost PLN 1,000 a year ago, today it will cost about PLN 1,040.

Keep in mind that it is an average. Your "personal inflation" can be higher or lower depending on what you spend on. If you drive a lot, rising fuel prices hit you harder. If you rent, rent increases matter most.

## Where does it come from?

There are three common causes:

- **more expensive raw materials and energy** – when oil or electricity gets pricier, the cost of producing and transporting almost everything rises,
- **strong demand** – when people and companies want to buy more than can be produced, prices go up,
- **rising wages and business costs** – companies pass part of their higher costs on to customers.

The central bank fights inflation – in Poland that is the NBP. Its main tool is interest rates: when it raises them, loans get more expensive, people borrow and spend less, and pressure on prices eases.

## What does inflation do to your savings?

This is the key point. If you keep PLN 10,000 under the mattress or in an account that pays no interest, and inflation is 4%, after a year your money will **buy what about PLN 9,600 buys today**. The number in your account hasn't changed, but its purchasing power has dropped.

That is why people say savings need "protecting from inflation". The simplest way is to keep them somewhere that pays interest – a savings account, a term deposit or government bonds. They won't always fully cover inflation, but they limit the loss.

## How to feel inflation less day to day

- **Check what you actually spend on.** Without that you don't know which price rises hurt you. Track your spending for at least a month.
- **Compare prices per kilo or litre**, not per pack – manufacturers often shrink packs instead of raising prices.
- **Review your fixed bills once a year**: internet, phone, insurance, subscriptions. Switching often saves hundreds a year.
- **Don't leave spare savings in a regular current account.** Even a basic savings account beats nothing.

## In short

- Inflation is rising prices – the same money buys less.
- The official figure is an average; your inflation depends on what you spend on.
- Money that earns no interest loses value – keep it where it earns some.
