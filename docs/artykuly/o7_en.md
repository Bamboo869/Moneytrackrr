How do I set a monthly budget?
A budget isn't a crash diet for your wallet – it's a plan that tells your money where to go. Here are five simple steps to your first budget, from working out your income to a monthly review that takes 10 minutes.
---
Budgeting sounds like tightening your belt and counting every penny. In practice it's simpler: **a plan for how much you want to spend, and on what, in a given month**. A good budget doesn't take the fun away – it stops you wondering where your pay went.

## Step 1: work out what you really get

Take the amount that actually lands in your account – **net**, after all deductions. If you have extra, irregular income, only put the certain part in your budget. Treat the rest as a bonus.

## Step 2: list your fixed costs

These are expenses that look similar every month:

- rent or mortgage,
- electricity, gas, internet, phone,
- insurance, memberships, subscriptions,
- loan payments and other fixed commitments.

Add them up. That's the amount that will "disappear" regardless of your choices.

## Step 3: estimate variable spending

Food, fuel, toiletries, going out, clothes. This is the hardest to guess, so it's best to **check your history from the last 1–3 months** – in your banking app or a tracker. It often turns out that eating out or small purchases cost twice what you thought.

Set a limit for each bigger category, e.g. food PLN 1,200, transport PLN 400, entertainment PLN 300.

## Step 4: plan savings like a bill

Put savings into your budget **as a fixed cost**, not "whatever is left". Even 5–10% of your income is a good start. Set up a transfer right after payday – then the budget itself makes sure that money doesn't get spent.

## Step 5: check weekly, review monthly

- **Once a week**, glance at how much is left in each category. That way you know mid-month whether you need to slow down.
- **Once a month**, compare the plan with reality. Where did you go over? Was the limit realistic? Adjust the amounts for next month.

Your first budget is almost never perfect. That's normal – after 2–3 months the limits start to fit your life.

## Common pitfalls

- **Forgetting yearly expenses** – car insurance, gifts, holidays. Divide them by 12 and set that aside every month.
- **Limits that are too strict** – a budget you can't stick to gets abandoned quickly.
- **No "other" category** – something unexpected always comes up.

## In short

- Start with your net income and fixed costs.
- Base variable spending limits on your history, not guesses.
- Treat savings like a bill and fine-tune your budget every month.
