Higher alcohol excise, sugar levy and VAT on drinks in Poland from 2027. How much more will you pay at the till?
Poland's Senate has passed three laws that, from 1 January 2027, would raise taxes on alcohol, sweetened drinks and some non-alcoholic drinks. We look at the new rates, estimate what it could cost a typical household and explain what may still change.
---
On Tuesday 27 October, Poland's Senate (the upper house of parliament) voted on three laws designed to bring more money to the **National Health Fund (NFZ)**, the public body that pays for healthcare. All three concern what goes into the shopping basket: alcohol, sweetened drinks and drinks with caffeine. If they are signed, the new rates will apply **from 1 January 2027**.

This is a second attempt. In December 2025, President Karol Nawrocki vetoed similar excise and sugar-levy rules, citing his pledge not to sign laws that raise taxes. So it is not yet certain that the increases will take effect.

## What the Senate passed

### 1. Higher excise duty on alcohol

Excise duty is a special tax added to the price of goods such as alcohol, fuel and cigarettes. The Sejm (the lower house) passed the amendment on 22 October (377 for, 59 against), and the Senate approved it **without amendments – 85 senators for, none against**. The law therefore goes straight to the President. Rates rise by about 15%:

- **ethyl alcohol (e.g. vodka):** from PLN 8,811 to **PLN 10,109** per hectolitre of pure alcohol,
- **beer:** from PLN 12.04 to **PLN 13.82** per hectolitre for each degree Plato (a measure of the beer's extract content),
- **wine and other fermented drinks:** from PLN 245 to **PLN 280.83** per hectolitre,
- **intermediate products (e.g. fortified wines):** from PLN 490 to **PLN 562.93** per hectolitre,
- **cider and perry up to 5% alcohol:** from PLN 97 to **PLN 116.40** per hectolitre.

The increase is bigger than the earlier excise "roadmap" had planned. The extra revenue is to go to the NFZ – the government plans to raise its subsidy to the fund by **PLN 1.4 billion a year** in 2027–2028.

### 2. Higher sugar levy

The sugar levy is paid by producers and sellers of drinks with added sugar or sweeteners. The Senate made one amendment to this law (62 for, 2 against, 19 abstentions), so it goes back to the Sejm first. The main changes:

- **fixed fee:** from PLN 0.50 to **PLN 0.70** per litre of drink (up to 5 g of sugars per 100 ml, or with sweeteners),
- **variable fee:** from PLN 0.05 to **PLN 0.10** for each gram of sugar above 5 g per 100 ml,
- **maximum fee:** from PLN 1.20 to **PLN 1.80** per litre,
- **added caffeine or taurine:** from PLN 0.10 to **PLN 1** per litre – ten times more,
- **new:** syrups and concentrates, including highly sweetened syrups sold as dietary supplements, charged at **PLN 3 per litre or kilogram** (except packs of up to 200 ml).

### 3. 23% VAT on some drinks

The third law (62 for, 0 against, 23 abstentions) scraps the reduced VAT rate for drinks with at least 20% juice in several categories. These include **energy drinks with juice, non-alcoholic beer, drinks with 0.0–0.5% alcohol, and non-alcoholic wines and ciders**. VAT will rise from **5% in shops and 8% in restaurants and bars to 23%**. The Sejm had earlier rejected an amendment that would have cut VAT on bottled water.

## What it could cost at the till

The laws set taxes, not shelf prices. Final prices will be set by producers and shops – they may pass on all of the increase, part of it or more. The figures below are therefore estimates, assuming the whole tax difference is passed on together with 23% VAT:

- **vodka 0.5 l (40%):** excise up by about PLN 2.60, with VAT about **PLN 3.19** more per bottle,
- **beer 0.5 l (12 degrees Plato):** about PLN 0.11, with VAT about **PLN 0.13** more per bottle or can,
- **wine 0.75 l:** about PLN 0.27, with VAT about **PLN 0.33** more per bottle,
- **sweetened drink without caffeine (10 g of sugar per 100 ml):** the levy rises from PLN 0.75 to PLN 1.20 per litre, so about **PLN 0.55** more per litre with VAT,
- **energy drink 0.5 l:** the caffeine or taurine fee alone rises by PLN 0.45 per can, about **PLN 0.55** with VAT – on top of the higher sugar fee.

Polish portal Wirtualna Polska gives similar estimates: about PLN 3 more for vodka, about 15 groszy (PLN 0.15) for beer and about 30 groszy for a bottle of wine.

## Example: a household budget

Take a household that buys, each month:

1. **12 beers of 0.5 l** – about PLN 1.58 more,
2. **2 bottles of wine** – about PLN 0.66 more,
3. **1 bottle of vodka (0.5 l)** – about PLN 3.19 more,
4. **8 litres of sweetened drinks without caffeine** – about PLN 4.43 more.

That adds up to about **PLN 9.90 a month**, or roughly **PLN 120 a year**. For some this is a small sum, for others it will be noticeable – especially in homes where sweetened drinks or energy drinks are part of every day. With two 0.5 l energy drinks a day, the higher caffeine fee alone could add more than PLN 30 a month.

## What happens next and what we don't know yet

- **The excise and VAT laws** now await the President's decision. He has 21 days to sign, veto or refer a law to the Constitutional Tribunal.
- **The sugar levy** goes back to the Sejm, which must consider the Senate's amendment. Only then will it reach the President.
- **It is not known whether the President will sign.** In December 2025 he vetoed similar rules.
- **It is not known how much prices will change.** Producers and shops may pass on all of the increase, only part of it, or more.

## What you can do

- **Check how much you really spend on drinks and alcohol.** These costs often disappear in a big grocery receipt. If you record them separately for a month – for example as a category in Money Trackrr – you will see whether the increase will cost you a few złoty or several dozen.
- **Don't stockpile "just in case".** Buying large amounts of alcohol before a tax rise ties up money and rarely pays off – especially as it is not yet certain the rules will take effect.
- **Compare the price per litre, not per pack.** After the changes, differences between brands and pack sizes may grow.
- **Take a closer look at energy drinks.** This is the category where the levy rises most. If you buy them daily, it is worth working out the yearly cost.
- **Consider cheaper alternatives.** Tap water, tea and drinks without sugar or caffeine are not covered by the new charges.

## In short

- On 27 October Poland's Senate passed laws that, from 1 January 2027, would raise excise duty on alcohol (by about 15%), the sugar levy and VAT on some juice-containing drinks (from 5% to 23%).
- The excise and VAT laws go to the President, while the sugar levy returns to the Sejm; in December 2025 the President vetoed similar rules.
- By estimates, a 0.5 l bottle of vodka could cost about PLN 3 more, beer about PLN 0.13–0.15, wine about PLN 0.30 and a litre of sweetened drink about PLN 0.55.
- For a household buying moderate amounts of alcohol and sweetened drinks each month, that is about PLN 120 a year; the extra revenue is meant to fund the NFZ.
