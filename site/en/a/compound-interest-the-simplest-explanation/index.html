Compound interest: the simplest explanation

25 October 2026·2 min read·Money Trackrr editorial team

Einstein supposedly called it the eighth wonder of the world. Here is how compound interest works in simple numbers, why time matters more than the amount, and how the same mechanism works against you with debt.

Compound interest is simply interest on interest. When you save and don't withdraw the interest you earn, the next period's interest is calculated on a bigger amount. At first the difference is small, but over time it snowballs.

A simple example

You deposit PLN 1,000 at 5% a year and withdraw nothing (we ignore tax to keep it simple):

  • after 1 year you have PLN 1,050,
  • after 2 years – PLN 1,102.50 (interest is now calculated on PLN 1,050),
  • after 10 years – about PLN 1,629,
  • after 20 years – about PLN 2,653,
  • after 30 years – about PLN 4,322.

Notice: the first 10 years added about PLN 629, the last 10 years more than PLN 1,600. The amount grows faster and faster, even though the rate never changes.

Time beats amount

Take two people. Anna saves PLN 200 a month from age 25, Bart saves PLN 400 a month but starts at 40. At the same interest rate Anna, despite paying in less in total, often ends up with a similar or bigger sum. Why? Her money had more years to "work".

The takeaway: it's better to start early with a little than late with a lot. Even PLN 50–100 a month makes a difference.

A quick trick: the rule of 72

Want to know how many years it takes to double your money? Divide 72 by the annual interest rate:

  • at 6% – about 12 years,
  • at 4% – about 18 years,
  • at 8% – about 9 years.

It's an approximation, but great for quick comparisons in your head.

The dark side: debt

The same mechanism works with debt – just against you. If you only pay the minimum on a credit card, unpaid interest is added to the balance, and interest is then charged on that too. That's why expensive debt is worth paying off as fast as possible, before you start saving seriously.

Things to remember

  • Tax on capital gains (19% in Poland) and inflation reduce your real return – calculator figures are usually "before" both.
  • Compounding frequency matters: interest added monthly grows slightly faster than interest added once a year.
  • Consistency matters more than the perfect moment to start.

In short

  • Compound interest is interest on interest – it speeds up over time.
  • Time is what matters most: start early, even with a small amount.
  • With debt it works against you – pay off expensive debt quickly.
