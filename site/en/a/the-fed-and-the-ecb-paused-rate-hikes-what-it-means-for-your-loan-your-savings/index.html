The Fed and the ECB paused rate hikes. What it means for your loan, your savings and the zloty ahead of Poland's rate decision

30 October 2026·8 min read·Money Trackrr editorial team

The US Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank left interest rates unchanged this week, but neither ruled out further hikes. We explain what this means for loans, deposits and exchange rates – and why next week matters most for people in Poland.

Within two days, the world's two most important central banks announced their decisions. On Wednesday 28 October, the Federal Reserve (Fed), the central bank of the United States, kept interest rates in the 3.75–4.00% range. A day later, the European Central Bank (ECB), which sets rates for the euro area, held its deposit rate at 2.50%.

Both decisions were in line with market expectations. What matters more is what the banks said about the future. Neither the Fed nor the ECB declared an end to rate hikes. Both point out that high energy prices could still push inflation up.

What exactly happened

The Fed: a pause after the September hike

In September, the Fed raised rates by 0.25 percentage points – its first hike in more than three years. In October it paused, but the decision was not unanimous. Ten committee members voted to hold, while two – Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack – wanted another hike.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said that "there is broad agreement that the monetary medicine is starting to work". However, he made no commitment about December. According to the broker XTB, the market saw the decision as milder than it had feared. The probability of a December hike priced in by investors fell from about 95% to about 85%. The Fed's next meeting is on 8–9 December.

The ECB: rates on hold, inflation risks tilted upwards

The ECB left all three key rates unchanged: the deposit rate at 2.50%, the main refinancing rate at 2.65% and the marginal lending rate at 2.90%. This is a pause after this year's hikes – the latest came in September. In its statement, the bank said energy prices are still rising and are "upside risks to the inflation outlook", and that financing conditions have "tightened" since its previous meeting.

ECB President Christine Lagarde summed it up briefly: "The risks to growth are tilted to the downside, the risks to inflation to the upside." The bank does not commit in advance to any particular rate path. According to a Bloomberg survey cited by FXMAG, most economists expect a hike in December, to 2.75%. The ECB's new forecasts will be published on 17 December.

Why it matters for Poland

The Fed and ECB decisions do not directly change your loan payment in Polish zloty (PLN). But they affect three things you can feel in your wallet.

  1. The zloty exchange rate. When rates in the US and the euro area rise faster than in Poland, investors are keener to hold dollars and euros. On Thursday morning, one US dollar cost about PLN 3.96 and one euro about PLN 4.41 (according to Managerplus). ING expects the euro to stabilise at around PLN 4.40–4.41.
  2. The decision of Poland's Monetary Policy Council. The Monetary Policy Council (RPP), which sets interest rates in Poland, meets on 3–4 November. The National Bank of Poland (NBP) reference rate currently stands at 3.75%. September inflation was 4.1%, above the NBP target (2.5% with a tolerance band of ±1 percentage point). The Council will also see the NBP's new inflation projection.
  3. Euro-denominated loans. People repaying a loan in euros have payments linked to EURIBOR, which follows ECB rates. The October pause means calm for now, but a December hike would raise these payments.

What economists say about the RPP

Forecasts are divided. Credit Agricole's base case is a one-off hike of 0.25 percentage points to 4.00%, although its economist Krystian Jaworski admits the chance of no change is "significant". ING expects a 0.5-point hike to around 4.25%, but only in the first quarter of 2027. mBank and PKO BP expect rates to stay at 3.75% until the end of 2026. Bank Millennium does not expect changes until at least the third quarter of 2027. The market is more nervous: according to 300Gospodarka, FRA contracts price 3-month WIBOR (the benchmark rate for most Polish variable-rate loans) at about 4.64% in six months.

Let's do the maths: how much a loan payment could change

Take a PLN 400,000 mortgage over 25 years with a variable rate: a bank margin of 2.0% plus 3-month WIBOR at around 3.88% (its mid-October level). The equal monthly payment is then about PLN 2,548.

  • a 0.25-point hike: about PLN 2,609, or roughly PLN 61 more per month (about PLN 730 a year),
  • a 0.5-point hike: about PLN 2,671, or roughly PLN 123 more (about PLN 1,480 a year),
  • WIBOR at the level priced by the market (4.64%): about PLN 2,736, or roughly PLN 188 more (about PLN 2,260 a year).

This is a simplified calculation – WIBOR reacts to expectations before the decision itself, and banks usually recalculate payments every 3 or 6 months.

And savings?

According to the NBP, new household deposits paid an average of 2.94% a year in August (as reported by finwire.pl). On PLN 20,000 over a year that is about PLN 588 in interest, or about PLN 476 after Poland's 19% capital gains tax (known as the "Belka tax"). With inflation at 4.1%, the same money loses about PLN 820 of purchasing power in a year. On an average deposit, you therefore lose about PLN 340 in real terms. The best offers reach about 6%, but they are usually for new customers, for a short period or up to a set amount.

What you can do

  • Check your loan agreement. See whether your rate is fixed or variable and when the bank recalculates your payment. Then you know when a possible hike will reach your budget.
  • Build a buffer for a higher payment. With a variable rate, set aside the difference from the calculation above each month. If there is no hike, you will be left with a financial cushion.
  • Don't keep savings in an account that pays no interest. Compare deposits, savings accounts and Polish treasury savings bonds. Read the terms: amount limits, promotion length, a requirement for new money.
  • Plan euro and dollar spending with a margin. If you pay for subscriptions or purchases in a foreign currency or are planning a trip, allow a few per cent for exchange-rate swings.
  • Keep track of fixed costs. When your loan payment, bills and subscriptions are in one place, for example as fixed payments in Money Trackrr, you can see straight away how much is left to spend after a payment change.

In short

  • The Fed (28 October) kept rates at 3.75–4.00%, and the ECB (29 October) held its deposit rate at 2.50%. Neither rules out further hikes, and markets expect them in December.
  • The decisions affect the zloty: on Thursday morning one dollar cost about PLN 3.96 and one euro about PLN 4.41.
  • Poland's Monetary Policy Council will decide on rates on 3–4 November. The reference rate is 3.75%, and economists are split between no change and a hike.
  • On a PLN 400,000 loan over 25 years, a 0.25-point hike means about PLN 61 more per month, and a 0.5-point hike about PLN 123.
