Poland's inflation falls to 3.5%. Cheaper fuel, pricier electricity and gas – what it means for your budget

31 October 2026·7 min read·Money Trackrr editorial team

According to a flash estimate from Statistics Poland, prices in October were 3.5% higher than a year ago, down from 4.1% in September. We explain where the drop came from, why economists are not calling an end to rising prices, and how to translate it into your own spending and savings.

On Friday 30 October, Statistics Poland (GUS, the country's official statistics office) published its flash estimate of October inflation. Prices of consumer goods and services were 3.5% higher than a year earlier. In September inflation stood at 4.1%, so the fall is clear.

The result was better than economists expected. The average forecast in a PAP Biznes survey was 3.7%, and according to the daily Rzeczpospolita even the lowest forecast was 3.6%. Compared with September, prices fell by 0.4%.

This brings inflation back to the upper edge of the target set by Narodowy Bank Polski (NBP, Poland's central bank). The NBP aims to keep inflation at 2.5%, with a tolerance band of 1 percentage point either way, i.e. between 1.5% and 3.5%.

Where the drop came from

The main reason is fuel. Since 3 October the government's "Ceny Paliwa Niżej" programme (CPN, "Lower Fuel Prices") is back: lower VAT and excise duty on petrol and diesel, plus maximum prices at filling stations. We wrote about it when new maximum prices took effect on 20 October.

The effect shows up in the GUS data:

  • Fuel for private cars: −9.7% in a month. It is still 21.7% more expensive than a year ago, though (in September the figure was as high as 36.1%).
  • Electricity, gas and other household fuels: +1.6% in a month and +6.0% year on year (5.0% in September).
  • Food and non-alcoholic beverages: +0.3% in a month but −0.1% year on year. Food costs roughly the same as a year ago.

According to Rzeczpospolita, without the CPN programme inflation would have been about 0.7 percentage points higher, i.e. around 4.2%.

Why economists are cautious

A flash estimate is only preliminary data. GUS will publish the full reading, including service prices, in mid-November, and the NBP will calculate core inflation (excluding food and energy prices) the following day. Economists at mBank estimate that core inflation may have fallen to about 3%. Economists at ING called the data "good news for the RPP".

Many analysts stress, however, that the drop is mainly thanks to lower fuel taxes rather than weaker price pressure in the economy.

"It is hard to speak of a lasting improvement," says Andrzej Gwiżdż of Port (quoted by Money.pl).

Łukasz Śliwka of VIG/C-QUADRAT TFI notes that "the structure of the flash estimate is not clearly disinflationary", because food is no longer pulling inflation down (quoted by Analizy.pl). Another key question is what happens when CPN expires – it is due to run until 31 December. Adam Antoniak of ING told Rzeczpospolita that the bank's baseline scenario assumes the programme will be extended to the end of February. Piotr Bartkiewicz of Pekao believes "it is reasonable to assume that regulated electricity and gas prices should rise by 10%".

What about interest rates?

The Monetary Policy Council (RPP), which sets interest rates in Poland, will announce its decision on 4 November. The NBP reference rate currently stands at 3.75%. mBank economists write that the decision "was (and still is) on a knife-edge", but the new data shift it "slightly in a dovish direction", i.e. towards no hike. Most economists quoted by Rzeczpospolita expect rates to stay unchanged. Piotr Arak of VeloBank warns, however, that the risk of a hike grows "with every dollar per barrel and every grosz of a weaker zloty".

The maths: what it means for a household budget

Inflation of 3.5% is an average for the whole basket. Your "personal" inflation depends on what you spend money on. Here is an example for a household that a year ago spent each month:

  • PLN 1,500 on food: today about PLN 1,498 (−0.1%), practically unchanged,
  • PLN 400 on electricity and gas: today about PLN 424 (+6%), PLN 24 more,
  • PLN 500 on fuel: today about PLN 609 (+21.7%), PLN 109 more.

Together these three items cost about PLN 131 more per month than a year ago, or about PLN 1,570 per year. Someone who drives a lot therefore feels rising prices more than the 3.5% average suggests.

Savings

With inflation at 3.5%, money that earns no interest loses value. According to finwire.pl, PLN 10,000 will after a year buy what about PLN 9,662 buys today – a loss of about PLN 338. For a deposit merely to keep up with prices after Poland's 19% capital gains tax (the so-called "Belka tax"), it needs to pay at least about 4.3% a year. Meanwhile, new deposits paid on average about 2.94% in August (NBP data via finwire.pl).

What you can do

  • Work out your own inflation. Compare what you spend on fuel, bills and food with a year ago. If you record spending by category, for example in Money Trackrr, just compare the same months.
  • Plan your fuel costs to the end of the year. Maximum prices under CPN apply until 31 December. It is not yet known whether the programme will be extended, so it makes sense to leave some margin for pricier fuel in your January budget.
  • Prepare for higher bills. Electricity and gas are rising faster than the rest of the basket. Check your tariff and consumption before the winter bills arrive.
  • Don't keep savings in an account that pays no interest. Compare deposits, savings accounts and treasury savings bonds. Look at the after-tax rate and compare it with inflation.
  • Watch the RPP decision on 4 November. If you have a variable-rate loan, a hike would raise your instalment. Setting aside a buffer for a higher payment is a sensible precaution.

In short

  • According to the GUS flash estimate, inflation in Poland fell to 3.5% in October from 4.1% in September – less than economists had forecast (3.7%).
  • The drop is mainly due to the CPN programme: fuel got 9.7% cheaper in a month, though it is still 21.7% pricier than a year ago. Electricity and gas are up 6% a year, while food costs the same as a year ago.
  • Economists warn that the improvement may be temporary, especially once CPN ends. The RPP will announce its interest rate decision on 4 November.
  • With inflation at 3.5%, a deposit has to pay about 4.3% before tax for savings not to lose value.
